# The Ponderwump — Pagination map (verse → page)

19 illustrated spreads. Each page = one illustration + the verse lines below. Never split a rhyming couplet across a page turn.

**P1** — Marnie introduced
> In a crooked little cottage at the edge of the wood / lived a girl with a head full of why, how, and could. / Her name, it was Marnie. Behind her bright eyes / spun a carousel wonder, forever surprise.

**P2** — Daddy loses his desk
> Her father made worlds out of letters and light, / till a hulking new machine took his desk overnight. / "It's quicker than you," the cold letter had read. / He came shuffling home rather grey, drooping his head.

**P3** — Strawberry joke
> But Marnie clambered up on his knee with a grin. / "That gadget's not clever. Now listen. Begin. / How many small R's are in strawberry, Dad?" / "The machine answered two." "Then the machine's rather bad, / for the answer is three!" And she spelled it with glee, / S, T, R, A, W, B, E, double-R, Y. / And Daddy, he chuckled, the first laugh all day, / for a warm, clever girl chased his grey cloud away.

**P4** — Mummy, the baby, the cloud
> Now Mummy sat wrapped in a blanket, unwell, / with a baby curled close, like a pip in its shell. / They kissed her soft cheek. "You're the dearest," they said. / (She still thought "the cloud" was the sky overhead.)

**P5** — Set off into the Muddlewood
> "Go wander," Mum murmured, "and let me lie low." / So they buttoned their coats and they went, off they go, / up over the hill to the Muddlewood, tall, / where the ferns wave like feathers and hush covers all.

**P6** — Belief vs doubt (refrain established)
> "In this forest," breathed Marnie, "a giant abides: / the Ponderwump, wisest of thinkers, who hides. / His thoughts are the biggest for miles, so they say." / Dad smiled, tired and kind. "That's a story, love. Play."

**P7** — Lost
> They rambled past toadstools and trickling streams, / till the path petered out and the light thinned to gleams. / "We're turned all about," fretted Daddy. "Oh dear." / But Marnie stood calm. "Then we'll think our way clear."

**P8** — Tortoise (sequencing) + fur detail
> By a shimmering brook sat a tortoise, forlorn, / all baffled by which mossy stone to step on. / "Just take them in order," said Marnie. "Watch how: / this one, then the next one, then that one. Go now. / So one at a time, tortoise, first, then the next." / He crossed with a beam, quite delighted, unvexed. / "My thanks, thoughtful girl. Here's a truth I can tell: / the Ponderwump's coat is deep moss, dusk as well, / a green touched with blue like the last of the day." / "You see?" glowed our Marnie. "He's real, come what may." / Dad ruffled her curls. "Or a dream, little one." / And onward they wandered, beneath the low sun.

**P9** — Ants (decomposition) + eyes detail
> A long line of ants heaved a crumb like a hill, / far too vast for their shoulders, they strained with a will. / "Then break it to pieces," urged Marnie. "Be smart. / One crumb becomes crumbs, and each ant takes a part. / A job that's too big, once you break it apart, / is a heap of small easy bits. That's the art." / They shared out the mountain and marched it away. / "A gift!" piped their captain. "For helping today: / the Ponderwump's eyes are two lanterns of amber, / that warm the cold dark like a coal in a chamber." / "You hear?" beamed our Marnie. "He's near. He is true." / Dad shook his head, smiling. "What a mind, love, on you."

**P10** — Squirrel (loops) + horns detail
> A squirrel shot past in a terrible fluster, / his acorns flung wide, all his hoard in a bluster. / "I built my nut-flinger too fast!" he confessed. / "I shipped it, I skipped it, I gave it no test, / and now the whole wood helps itself to my store!" / "Then gather them gently," said Marnie. "Once more, / do one, set it down, then do one thing again, / repeat it and repeat it, and count up to ten. / Keep doing it over, no fuss and no stooping." / He scampered about, nut by nut, no more drooping. / "For that," puffed the squirrel, "a secret I've spied: / the Ponderwump's horns curl like questions, held high." / "He's close, I can feel it!" cried Marnie. "He's here!" / Dad blinked. For a moment, the tale felt quite near.

**P11** — Owl (algorithm) + tail & specs, Daddy nearly believes
> An owl on a bough hooted, ruffled and pale, / "So many ways homeward, I dither and fail." / "Then plan it," said Marnie. "Write steps in a row: / step one, and step two, and step three. **P12** — The black river
> I believe it. I do." But the trees opened wide / on a river of black, roaring, deep, swift and snide. / Too broad and too bitter to paddle across. / Poor Daddy sank down with a shivery loss. / "We're stuck, little Marnie, whatever we do."

**P13** — The duck (rubber-duck), the plan forms
> But a duck on a stump gave a sleepy "just you." / So Marnie described, while it blinked on its log, / every twist of the trouble, aloud through the fog, / and naming each knot of it, out in the air, / made the answer stand up, plain and clear, waiting there. / "I have it!" she cried. "We shall borrow each rule: / lay flat stones in order, all steady, all cool, / break the wide, rushing river to hops, one by one, / place a stone, then another, repeat till it's done. / A plan, start to finish, a bridge we can make." / But the stones were too heavy to lift or to break.

**P14** — THE REVEAL
> Then the ferns gave a shiver, the shadows all stirred, / and a hum, deep and gentle, was suddenly heard. / Up rose from the gloom, taller far than the trees, / a coat the soft green of the moss, edged in seas / of a dusky blue shimmer; two lanterns of amber / that glowed through the night like a fire in a chamber; / two horns curling up into shy little questions; / a long looping tail (just the owl's very mention); / and, perched on his snout with the daintiest care, / two small round old spectacles, thoughtful and fair.

**P15** — Daddy believes
> "The Ponderwump! Look!" Marnie gasped. "There he stands!" / And Daddy just stared, his doubt slipping his hands. / "He's real," Daddy breathed. "All this time you were right. / My clever, brave girl, you could see through the night."

**P16** — The muddle & the plan
> But the great gentle giant slumped down with a moan, / "I've wandered for years, cold and muddled, alone. / My thoughts are so many they knot and they tie. / I ponder and ponder, then muddle, and sigh." / So Marnie stood tall by his enormous left toe. / "We'll think it together. Just steady. Just slow. / First order our stones, then we break the black wide / into hops, and repeat them from this side to side. / Then you, gentle giant, lie down as a road, / lay your tail to the far bank and shoulder the load."

**P17** — The bridge / carried home
> The Ponderwump brightened. "A plan! Oh, how grand! / No more need I muddle, if thinking is planned." / He arched his huge back into one mighty span, / laid his tail like a bridge, and across them he ran, / well, carried them, cradled, as soft as a sigh, / back over the water while stars swept the sky. / The cottage glowed gold through the dark and the mist, / and there in the window stood Mummy, moon-kissed.

**P18** — Reunion + Daddy's healing
> "You found one!" laughed Mummy. "A friend, oh how dear!" / And she hugged them both close as the giant peeped near. / Then Daddy knelt down by his girl in the glow. / "A machine took my chair. But now, Marnie, I know: / a mind is a garden no engine can grow. / I'll wonder, I'll build, I'll imagine, I'll try, / and I'll teach the new baby to question the sky."

**P19** — Marnie asleep
> So the Ponderwump curled in the ferns by their door, / a friend to the family for evermore. / And Marnie slept deep, with the moon on her brow, / and a thought of her own, glowing warm. Wonder how.
